Whenever the number of bits in the digital signal exceeds the capacity of the registers,
overflow occurs. This can happen when two binary numbers are added or multiplied.
Overflow can also occur in the A/D conversion process when the input signal’s
amplitude exceeds the maximum range of the ADC. The overflow of signals results in a
number of errors, namely, overflow limit cycles, product quantisation noise or saturation
noise. Once these errors are detected they can be easily handled, however, the most
efficient approach is to prevent these errors from occurring. This can be done by scaling
the input at certain points in the digital filter. Scaling must be done in such a way that no
overflow occurs at the summing point (accumulator) of the digital filter.
Scaling in digital filters involves multiplying signals or coefficients with appropriate
factors to ensure that the internal states of the filter do not exceed the allowable numeric
range, thereby preventing overflow. FIR filters require only input scaling, while IIR
filters require detailed internal scaling of each second-order section due to feedback and
potential internal signal growth. Scaling does not affect the frequency response but
ensures safe and efficient fixed-point implementation.
Limit cycle oscillations are unwanted, self-sustained oscillations that occur in fixed-
point digital filters even when the input is zero. They arise due to the nonlinear effects of
quantization in the arithmetic operations of the filter.
They are especially significant in IIR filters because of feedback loops.
